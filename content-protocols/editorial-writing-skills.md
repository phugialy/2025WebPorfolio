# Editorial Writing Skills

These skills help the writer and editor decide what the article should become before rewriting it.

## 1. Comprehension Skill

Before editing or drafting, identify:

- What the article is really about
- Who it currently speaks to
- The strongest idea
- The weakest section
- Sections that do not belong
- The assumption the reader may be making

## 2. Article Type Classifier

Classify the article before choosing structure:

- Executive insight
- Critical thinking piece
- Business warning
- Technical explainer
- Practical guide
- Opinion/editorial
- Workflow tutorial
- Strategic memo

Do not force a guide structure onto an insight, warning, or critical thinking piece.

## 3. Perspective Reframe Skill

Apply the correct role lens:

- CFO
- Founder
- CTO
- Engineering manager
- Operations manager
- Business owner
- Investor
- Local commercial operator

The role changes what matters. A CFO lens prioritizes cost visibility, budget leakage, ROI, governance, risk, and control. An engineering lens can prioritize implementation, architecture, review gates, and reliability.

## 4. Research Trigger Skill

Research only when external facts are needed.

Research is needed for:

- Current pricing
- Market claims
- product details
- recent benchmarks
- statistics
- external evidence

Research is not required for:

- structure
- tone
- audience fit
- hook writing
- transitions
- internal logic
- role-based reframing

If research is needed but unavailable, label the gap instead of inventing support.

## 5. Editorial Hook Skill

Write hooks that create reader realization, not generic explanation.

Weak:

- AI token usage is an important cost consideration.

Stronger:

- Your team may not be saving money with AI. It may just be moving waste from payroll into token spend.

The hook should reveal tension, risk, cost, missed assumption, or a decision the reader has avoided.

## 6. Structure Freedom Skill

The writer and editor are allowed to remove and restructure sections.

- Remove "Try this next" if the article is not a how-to.
- Remove "What I would avoid" if it feels forced.
- Replace tactical sections with strategic sections for executive audiences.
- Preserve only sections that serve the article type, audience, and reader effect.

## 7. Transition and Flow Skill

Improve movement between ideas:

- tension
- contrast
- escalation
- consequence
- reader realization

Each section should make the next section feel necessary.

## 8. Critical Thinking Skill

For critical thinking pieces, ask:

- What assumption is the reader making?
- What are they not seeing?
- What is the uncomfortable truth?
- What changes if this is true?
- What decision should this pressure?

The goal is the "oh, I have not thought about it that way" moment.
